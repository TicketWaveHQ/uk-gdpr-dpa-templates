# International transfer addendum (UK IDTA) cover note

> **Not legal advice.** This explains when you need the UK International Data Transfer Addendum and gives a short cover note to sit alongside it. The IDTA itself is a form published by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO); use the current official version. Take qualified advice on any cross-border transfer.

---

## When you need this

You need a transfer mechanism whenever personal data covered by the UK GDPR is sent to, or accessed from, a country outside the United Kingdom. In plain terms:

- If your sub-processor hosts the data **in the UK**: no mechanism needed.
- If it hosts the data in a country the UK has recognised as **adequate** (this includes the EEA at the time of writing): you rely on that adequacy. Record which one.
- If it hosts the data anywhere else (for example the **United States**): you need contractual protection. The usual route from the UK is the **International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses** ("IDTA"), or the standalone UK IDTA form.

Always check the current position, because adequacy decisions change.

## What the IDTA is

The IDTA is the UK's answer to Standard Contractual Clauses. It is a fill-in form, published by the ICO, that binds the party receiving the data to protect it to a UK standard. You attach it to your contract with the receiving party (often the sub-processor's own DPA already incorporates it, in which case you rely on theirs and record that).

## Before you rely on the IDTA: the risk assessment

Signing the IDTA is not the whole job. UK guidance expects you to carry out a **transfer risk assessment** (TRA): a short, honest look at whether the protections in the IDTA will actually hold up given the destination country's laws and the sensitivity of the data. The ICO publishes a TRA tool. Keep your assessment on file. For most small SaaS transfers of low-risk data to a mainstream US provider, this is a proportionate, documented judgement, not a legal treatise, but it must be done and kept.

## Cover note (attach to the IDTA or to your DPA)

> **International transfer cover note**
>
> This note records the basis on which [PROCESSOR / YOUR COMPANY] transfers personal data outside the United Kingdom in providing [SERVICE] to [CONTROLLER].
>
> 1. **Transfers.** Personal data described in Schedule 1 of the Data Processing Agreement is processed by the sub-processors listed in Schedule 2. For each sub-processor hosting data outside the UK, the country and mechanism are stated there.
> 2. **Mechanism.** Where the destination is not covered by UK adequacy regulations, the transfer is made under the UK International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, [in the version dated [DATE] / as incorporated in the sub-processor's own terms dated [DATE]], which is incorporated into this arrangement by reference.
> 3. **Risk assessment.** A transfer risk assessment has been completed for each such transfer and is available to the Controller on request.
> 4. **Review.** This note and the underlying assessments are reviewed at least annually, and whenever a relevant adequacy decision or sub-processor changes.
>
> Signed: [NAME], [TITLE], [DATE].
